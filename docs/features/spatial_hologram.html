👤 2. Glasses-Free Holographic Lightfields & Lifelike Human Personas

How can a holographic display project out of a flat screen and take the lifelike shape of a real person without requiring bulky 3D headsets? Nyx integrates three foundational technologies:

A. Real-Time Volumetric Human Capture

To render a photorealistic human representation in real time, standard 2D video feeds are insufficient. Nyx utilizes:

  • TrueDepth Infrared Point-Cloud Sensor: Emits over 30,000 structured infrared dots onto the speaker's face and upper torso, measuring millimeter-accurate depth contours ($Z$) at 120 FPS.
  • 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) Compression: Instead of dense, rigid polygon meshes that cause uncanny valley effects, the person is modeled as hundreds of thousands of semi-transparent, anisotropic 3D Gaussian ellipsoids. This captures hair strands, subtle facial muscle micro-expressions, skin translucency, and subsurface scattering with zero CPU lag.
  • Sub-8ms Neural Facial Deformation: An on-device NPU model predicts eye gaze convergence and lip sync geometry directly from the raw microphone and IR stream, transmitting only compact spatial deformation coefficients across the network (< 45 KB/s).

B. Diffractive Nano-Metalens Optical Layer

To project light outward into space without 3D glasses, the display employs a switchable Nano-Metalens Parallax Steerer over a high-density Micro-OLED panel:

// std/mobile/hologram.nyx — Optical Frustum & Eye Convergence let hologram = HologramEngine.new(views: 4); // 4-View Lightfield // Lock pupil coordinates from 120 FPS front IR camera hologram.update_gaze_lock(EyeGazeCoordinate { left_pupil_x: -31.5, left_pupil_y: 12.0, right_pupil_x: 31.5, right_pupil_y: 12.0, distance_cm: 36.2, is_converged: true }); // Single-pass GPU Multiview Instancing (< 4.15ms render time) let render_time = hologram.render_lightfield_frame(scene_node_count: 128);

As the user's head moves relative to the laptop screen, the front camera tracks pupil coordinates at 120Hz. The optical layer steers different perspective rays into the left and right eyes respectively, creating the optical perception of a floating 3D human head hovering 5 to 20 cm above the laptop keyboard.

C. Mid-Air Hover Interaction (Touch in Thin Air)

Using front-facing Time-of-Flight (ToF) sensors, the system establishes a 3D interaction boundary above the display glass. When the user hovers their hand in the holographic light cone:

  • Elevation Raycasting (2cm - 15cm): Measures finger altitude above the glass to detect hover states, micro-pinches, and rotational gestures.
  • Ultrasound Mid-Air Haptics: Phased ultrasonic transducers focus acoustic radiation pressure onto the fingertip, allowing the user to literally feel the tactile resistance of the floating holographic object in empty air.
